**Arizona now has four free tools that warn you if someone tries to steal your property, but each one watches a different moment in the process.** Below you'll find a side-by-side comparison, sign-up steps for Pinal and Maricopa County owners, and a plain-English breakdown of **Senate Bill 1479**, the deed fraud law that took effect in September 2026.

## The Short Answer

**Sign up for at least two:** the state's **ERAS** alert (catches a sale *before* it closes) and your **county recorder** alert (catches a document *after* it's recorded). If your property is in **Pinal County**, add the Assessor's **PAL** program too.

## The 4 Free Alerts at a Glance

| Alert | Run by | Tracks by | Fires when | Sign up |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **ERAS** (Early Real Estate Sales Alert System) | Arizona Department of Real Estate | Name, address or APN | An escrow officer enters a **pending sale** | Online at earlyalert.azre.gov |
| **Pinal County Recording Notification** | Pinal County Recorder | Name | A document is **recorded** under that name | Online |
| **Maricopa Title Alert** | Maricopa County Recorder | Name (person or business) | A document is **recorded** under that name (email and/or text) | Online |
| **Parcel Alert List (PAL)** | Pinal County Assessor | Parcel number | The Assessor gets a **new deed** or **mailing address change** | **In person** with photo ID |

## Which Alerts Apply to Your Address?

- **San Tan Valley, Johnson Ranch, Pecan Creek, Ironwood Crossing, Florence area:** Pinal County. Use **ERAS + Pinal Recording Notification + PAL**.
- **Queen Creek:** The town sits in **both counties**. Look up your parcel on the Pinal or Maricopa Assessor site first.
  - **Maricopa side:** **ERAS + Maricopa Title Alert**
  - **Pinal side** (*confirm with your parcel, since the county line runs through town*): **ERAS + Pinal Recording Notification + PAL**
- **Own land in both counties?** Sign up with both recorders.

## How Each One Works, and What It Can't Do

### ERAS: The Only One That Can Warn You Before Closing

The **Arizona Department of Real Estate** launched ERAS in late August 2026. When an escrow officer enters a pending sale that matches your registered property, you get a **text and email** with a link to report it as unauthorized.

**Limits:** It only works when escrow officers enter the transaction, and the Department says the database isn't a complete registry of every Arizona property. It won't catch a forged deed that skips escrow entirely.

**Seller tip:** ADRE has a transaction form sellers can sign so escrow sends their sale details into ERAS. If you're listing with us, ask about it.

### County Recorder Alerts: Your Paper Trail Watchdog

Both recorders email you when any document (deed, lien, deed of trust) is recorded under a name you monitor.

**Limits:**
- They only report documents recorded **after** you sign up.
- They track **names**, so a common last name can mean extra emails.
- By the time you're notified, the document **is already recorded**. You'll still need to act fast.
- Privacy note: under Arizona public records law, subscriber email addresses in Maricopa's system have been reported as potentially disclosable. Consider using a dedicated email.

### Pinal County PAL: A Human Double-Check

**PAL** is the only one tied to your **parcel number**, not your name. Once you enroll in person, the **Pinal County Assessor's Office** contacts you when it receives a new deed or a request to change your mailing address. Changing the mailing address is a classic early move by fraudsters, because it quietly redirects your tax bills.

**Limits:** It can't stop a deed from being recorded, and you have to visit an Assessor's office to enroll.

## Sign-Up Checklist (15 Minutes)

1. ☐ Find your **APN** on your tax bill or the county Assessor website.
2. ☐ Register at **earlyalert.azre.gov** (ERAS).
3. ☐ Register with your **county recorder's** notification service.
4. ☐ Monitor **every name on title**: each owner, your **trust**, your **LLC**.
5. ☐ Pinal owners: bring ID to an **Assessor's office** for PAL.
6. ☐ Add the alert senders to your contacts so emails don't land in spam.
7. ☐ Put a reminder on your calendar to **update your email** if it changes.

## What SB 1479 Changed

Governor Katie Hobbs signed **SB 1479** on April 9, 2026. It took effect in September.

### At the Recorder's Counter
- **Photo ID is required** to record a document in person. **Pinal County began requiring it on September 14, 2026.**
- Accepted IDs include an Arizona driver license or ID card, another state's license, a U.S. passport, military ID, tribal ID and a green card.
- **Exempt submitters:** escrow and title companies, banks and credit unions, Arizona State Bar members, and government agencies.

### At the Notary
- Notaries must take a **thumbprint** in their journal for deeds and other key real estate documents.
- **Remote online notarizations** are exempt from the thumbprint only if the notary keeps the **video for at least seven years** and logs the signer's ID number.

### In Court
- **Knowingly recording a forged or false claim** is now a **felony**, up from a class 1 misdemeanor.
- Victims can recover **at least $5,000 or triple actual damages**, whichever is greater, plus attorney fees.
- The law **repealed the five-year rule** at the center of *Dominguez v. Dominguez*, which could let an uncontested forged deed stand.

## Why Bother If the Law Is Stronger Now?

Because fraud is growing faster than enforcement. The FBI's IC3 recorded **$275.1 million** in real estate fraud losses in 2025. The Arizona Attorney General's Office has received **nearly 400 real estate fraud complaints since 2024**. Laws punish thieves after the fact. Alerts let **you** catch them first.

## Key Takeaways

- **ERAS** warns you **before** a sale closes. Recorder alerts warn you **after** a document records.
- **Queen Creek** owners must check which county their parcel is in.
- **Pinal's PAL** watches your **parcel number** and mailing address changes.
- **SB 1479** added photo ID, notary thumbprints, felony penalties and ended the five-year loophole.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the Arizona Early Real Estate Sales Alert System?

ERAS is a free tool from the Arizona Department of Real Estate. You register your property at earlyalert.azre.gov, and it texts and emails you if an escrow officer enters a pending sale on it, giving you time to stop a fraudulent closing.

### How do I sign up for Pinal County deed fraud alerts?

Register online for the Pinal County Recorder's Recording Notification service, and visit a Pinal County Assessor's office with photo ID to enroll in the Parcel Alert List (PAL). Add ERAS for pre-closing warnings.

### Is Maricopa Title Alert free?

Yes. Maricopa Title Alert is free and sends an email or text when a document is recorded under a name you monitor with the Maricopa County Recorder.

### Which county is Queen Creek in?

Queen Creek is in both Maricopa and Pinal County. Check your parcel on the Assessor websites to know which recorder alert to use.

### Do I need ID to record a deed in Arizona now?

Yes. Since September 2026, SB 1479 requires a valid government photo ID to record a document in person, unless you're an exempt professional such as an escrow officer, title agent, bank or attorney.
