**An empty house is a quiet invitation to three problems: someone "selling" it out from under you, someone moving into it, and an insurance policy that stops covering it.** This guide covers all three for snowbirds, heirs, landlords between tenants and anyone who's already moved but hasn't sold. It includes Arizona's new **SB 1426** squatter law, which took effect **September 12, 2026**.

## Who This Is For

- **Snowbirds** who leave for the summer
- **Families handling an inherited home** after a loved one passes
- **Owners who moved into new construction** before selling the old house
- **Landlords** between tenants
- **Out-of-state owners** holding a rental

## Risk 1: Deed Fraud and Fake Sales

The **American Land Title Association's 2026 study** found fraudsters are moving beyond vacant land. **Vacation homes (51%)** and **rental properties (48%)** are now common targets, and **more than half** of title firms said **properties of recently deceased owners** are frequently targeted.

**What to do:**
1. **Register for ERAS**, the Arizona Department of Real Estate's free pre-closing alert, at **earlyalert.azre.gov**.
2. **Sign up for your county recorder alert:** Pinal County Recording Notification or Maricopa Title Alert.
3. **Pinal County owners:** enroll in the Assessor's **Parcel Alert List (PAL)** in person.
4. **Inherited home?** Get the **probate or estate paperwork** moving and make sure the property is registered under the right name. A home left in a deceased owner's name is an easy mark.

## Risk 2: Squatters

### What Changed with SB 1426

Governor Katie Hobbs signed **Senate Bill 1426** on **May 29, 2026**. It took effect **September 12, 2026**, and creates a **faster court process** to remove **unauthorized occupants**.

- It covers people with **no lease, no verbal or written agreement**, who aren't **current or former tenants** or **immediate family** of the owner.
- Once the court rules for the owner, the **writ of restitution issues immediately**, instead of after the usual waiting period.
- Officials involved with the law have said removal could take **days rather than weeks**.
- **Tenant protections don't change.** If the person ever had permission to live there, the normal eviction process applies.

### What Still Hasn't Changed
- **No self-help.** You **can't change the locks, shut off utilities or remove belongings** to force someone out. That can make you the defendant.
- **The "30-day squatter's rights" idea is a myth.** Claiming ownership through **adverse possession** in Arizona takes years of open, continuous occupation, typically **10 years** under the general statute.

**If you find someone in your home:** Call law enforcement, don't confront the person, document everything and talk to an attorney about filing under the new process.

## Risk 3: Your Insurance May Quietly Shrink

Many standard homeowners policies **limit or exclude coverage** (vandalism, broken glass, water damage) once a home has been **vacant for a set period, often around 60 consecutive days**. Every policy is different.

**What to do:**
- **Call your insurance agent before you leave** and ask about the vacancy clause.
- Ask about a **vacancy endorsement** or a **vacant home policy**.
- Keep proof of **regular inspections**.

## The Vacant Home Checklist

### Make It Look Lived-In
- ☐ Keep **landscaping and irrigation** on a timer. Dead grass and overgrowth signal "empty."
- ☐ Put **interior lights** on smart timers or plugs.
- ☐ **Stop mail** or forward it, and ask a neighbor to grab packages and flyers.
- ☐ Add **camera doorbells** and **smart locks** you can check from your phone.

### Protect the Systems
- ☐ Set the **thermostat** high enough to protect the home in summer but low enough to avoid overworking the A/C. Ask your HVAC pro for a setting.
- ☐ **Shut off the main water** or install a **leak sensor and auto shutoff valve**.
- ☐ Unplug electronics and check the **pool pump** schedule if you have a pool.

### Have Eyes On It
- ☐ Arrange **walk-throughs at least every two weeks** by a neighbor, property manager or house-watch service.
- ☐ Give your **HOA** your contact info and dates away.
- ☐ Keep a list of who has keys.

### Watch the Paper Trail
- ☐ **ERAS** + **county recorder alert** + **PAL** (Pinal)
- ☐ Check your **property tax bill** arrives. If it stops, find out why.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How do I protect my vacant home in Arizona?

Register for ERAS and your county recorder alert, make the home look lived-in with timers and landscaping, schedule walk-throughs, shut off or monitor water, and confirm your insurance covers vacancy.

### What is Arizona's new squatter law?

SB 1426 took effect September 12, 2026. It creates a faster court process to remove unauthorized occupants who have no lease, agreement or family relationship with the owner, with a writ of restitution issued immediately after judgment.

### Can I change the locks if a squatter is in my house in Arizona?

No. Arizona prohibits self-help evictions like changing locks or cutting utilities. Use the court process, now faster under SB 1426.

### Does homeowners insurance cover a vacant house?

Often only partly. Many policies limit coverage after a home is vacant for a set period, frequently around 60 days. Ask your agent about a vacancy endorsement or vacant home policy.

### Are squatters' rights 30 days in Arizona?

No. That's a myth. Adverse possession in Arizona generally requires years of continuous, open occupation, typically 10 years.
